# Market Size, Growth, and Demand Signals for AI Automation Services (as of Sept 2026)

Source-quality legend: **[Tier 1]** = government statistics, Gartner, McKinsey, or company filings. **[Tier 2]** = VC/bank surveys with disclosed methodology (Menlo, Goldman Sachs, Bluevine). **[Tier 3 / LOW RELIABILITY]** = paid market-research report mills (Grand View, MarketsandMarkets, Research and Markets, Market.us, Fortune Business Insights, Meticulous, and similar). Their numbers often disagree by 2x or more and are rarely audited. Blog aggregators are noted as such.

## 1. Market size and CAGR: workflow automation / IPA, AI agents, GenAI services, hyperautomation

### Takeaway
Every source agrees the category is growing fast, but the dollar figures vary by an order of magnitude depending on scope. The most credible top-down number is Gartner's: **AI services worth about $576.5B in 2026**, inside total AI spending of $2.5–2.7T. The narrower "AI agents" software market is about **$8B (2025), rising to about $50B by 2030 at roughly 46% CAGR** according to the report mills, and Gartner puts "AI agents & assistants" spending at **$29.2B in 2026**. Plain workflow automation software is a slower, mature market, growing about 10–22%/yr.

### Cited Findings
**Gartner: total AI spending, including services [Tier 1]**
- Gartner (Sept 16, 2026): worldwide AI spending will total about **$2.7T in 2026, up 49.5% YoY**. Gartner has raised this figure several times during 2026: $2.5T in January 2026, then a "47% growth" forecast in May 2026, then $2.67–2.7T in September — [Gartner Sept 2026](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-09-16-gartner-forecasts-worldwide-ai-spending-to-grow-49-point-5-percent-in-2026); [Gartner Jan 2026](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-1-15-gartner-says-worldwide-ai-spending-will-total-2-point-5-trillion-dollars-in-2026); [Gartner May 2026](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-05-19-gartner-forecasts-worldwide-ai-spending-to-grow-47-percent-in-2026)
- Gartner's 2026 split by segment: Infrastructure $1.484T (about 56%); **AI Services $576.5B**; AI Software $461.6B; **AI Agents & Assistants $29.2B**; GenAI models $28.3B. Total 2025 spending was $1.79T. — [THE Journal summarizing Gartner, Sept 21 2026](https://thejournal.com/articles/2026/09/21/gartner-worldwide-ai-spending-to-reach-2-67-trillion-in-2026.aspx)
- Gartner estimates the AI services opportunity at **$1.2T by 2030**. This comes via a secondary summary; I could not open the Gartner primary page (HTTP 403) — [Techstrong.ai](https://techstrong.ai/agentic-ai/gartner-forecasts-worldwide-ai-spending-to-surge-to-2-7-trillion-in-2026/)
- Gartner hyperautomation: the market for "hyperautomation via process-agnostic software" reaches **$119.2B by 2028 at a 15.9% CAGR**. Another often-quoted Gartner figure puts "hyperautomation-enabling software" at about $1.04T by 2026. That scope is very broad (it counts adjacent software), so do not treat it as a services TAM — [Gartner doc 6165623](https://www.gartner.com/en/documents/6165623) (from the search snippet only; the document is paywalled)

**AI agents [Tier 3]**
- MarketsandMarkets: AI agents market grows from **$7.84B (2025) to $52.62B (2030), a 46.3% CAGR**. Vertical AI agents are the fastest-growing segment at a 62.7% CAGR — [MarketsandMarkets press release, Aug 2026](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/08/20/3348589/0/en/ai-agents-market-surges-to-52-62-billion-at-a-cagr-46-3-by-2030-report-by-marketsandmarkets.html)
- Grand View Research: **$50.31B by 2030 at a 45.8% CAGR** (2025–2030), and $182.97B by 2033 (49.6% CAGR, 2026–2033) — [PR Newswire / Grand View](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/ai-agents-market-size-to-hit-50-31-billion-by-2030-at-cagr-45-8---grand-view-research-inc-302447060.html); [Grand View](https://www.grandviewresearch.com/press-release/global-ai-agents-market-report)
- The two report mills agree closely on AI agents (about $50B by 2030). Gartner's $29.2B figure for 2026 "agents & assistants" spending is much higher than the roughly $11B the mills' 2026 curves imply, which shows how differently each source defines the category.

**Enterprise GenAI spend: application layer [Tier 2, VC]**
- Menlo Ventures (Dec 2025, **2025 data**): US enterprises spent **$37B on GenAI in 2025, up from $11.5B in 2024 (3.2x)** and $1.7B in 2023. $19B went to the application layer. Horizontal copilots took $8.4B, departmental AI $7.3B (of which coding was $4.0B), and vertical AI $3.5B (of which healthcare was $1.5B) — [Menlo Ventures](https://menlovc.com/perspective/2025-the-state-of-generative-ai-in-the-enterprise/); [GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2025/12/09/3202258/0/en/menlo-ventures-2025-state-of-generative-ai-report-enterprise-investment-hit-37b-in-2025-tripling-in-one-year.html)
- Menlo: **76% of enterprise AI solutions are bought rather than built (up from 53% in 2024)**. 47% of AI deals reach production, compared with 25% for traditional SaaS. Menlo did not break out services or consulting spend — [Menlo Ventures](https://menlovc.com/perspective/2025-the-state-of-generative-ai-in-the-enterprise/)

**AI consulting / services**
- Accenture [Tier 1, company-reported, via secondary summary]: cumulative advanced-AI bookings of **$11.5B through Q1 FY2026**, with $4.8B cumulative revenue. In Q1 FY26 alone it booked $2.2B (+76% YoY) and recognized $1.1B revenue (+120% YoY), across 1,300+ clients. I took these figures from a secondary summary and did not verify them against Accenture's filings — [Luminix summary](https://www.useluminix.com/reports/company-overviews/what-will-ai-do-to-accenture-s-business/source/0)
- Fortune Business Insights [Tier 3]: AI consulting services market grows from **$11.91B (2026) to $73.89B (2034), a 25.6% CAGR** — [Fortune Business Insights](https://www.fortunebusinessinsights.com/ai-consulting-services-market-111179)

**Workflow automation / IPA [Tier 3; wide disagreement]**
- Research and Markets (Mordor-style scope): workflow automation is **$23.77B (2025), reaching $37.45B (2030) at a 9.52% CAGR** — [Research and Markets](https://www.researchandmarkets.com/reports/5026146/workflow-automation-market-share-analysis)
- Meticulous Research: **$77.8B by 2030 at a 21.3% CAGR** (2023–2030) — [Meticulous](https://www.meticulousresearch.com/pressrelease/940/workflow-automation-market)
- Intelligent Process Automation: $17.95B (2025) to $38.96B (2030) at a 16.7% CAGR (Business Research Co., via OpenPR) — [OpenPR](https://www.openpr.com/news/4634164/intelligent-process-automation-market-analysis-highlights). Market.us gives $18.52B (2025) to $265.31B (2035) at a 30.5% CAGR — [Market.us](https://market.us/report/ai-powered-intelligent-process-automation-ipa-market/)
- Hyperautomation (Business Research Co.): $64.78B (2025) to $75.54B (2026), about 16.6% — [OpenPR](https://www.openpr.com/news/4634638/hyperautomation-market-intelligence-report-covers-trends)

### Inferences
- Workflow automation estimates for 2030 range from $37B to $78B (a 2x gap), and IPA CAGRs range from 16.7% to 30.5%. Treat report-mill figures as directional only; do not use them in a pitch deck as though they were precise.
- Services are a large share of AI spending. Gartner's AI services ($576.5B) exceed AI software ($461.6B) in 2026. Most of that services money, though, flows to large systems integrators (Accenture and similar) serving enterprises, not to small agencies.
- Menlo's finding that 76% of solutions are bought signals that buyers prefer packaged products to custom builds. That is a headwind for bespoke automation builders unless they resell, implement, or configure products.

### Gaps
- There is no credible, audited figure for the SMB-specific AI implementation services market, nor any estimate of total "AI automation agency" revenue. I found no source for either.
- I could not find an IDC agentic or AI services figure for 2026 in the time available.
- Gartner primary pages returned HTTP 403, so segment figures come from press coverage (THE Journal, Techstrong).

## 2. SMB AI adoption rates (2025–2026) and barriers

### Takeaway
It depends on who you ask. The official Census BTOS puts AI use at **about 17–20% of US firms** (December 2025 to May 2026). Adoption is flat for firms with fewer than 20 employees and rising only among larger firms. Vendor and bank surveys of engaged owners report **68–76% "use AI,"** but only **about 14% have it fully embedded** in operations. The main blockers are data security (33%), distrust of accuracy (31%), cost (24%) and lack of expertise and training (73% want more training).

### Cited Findings
**Government [Tier 1]**
- Census BTOS: overall AI use was **17–20% (December 14, 2025 to May 3, 2026)**, and 20–23% expect to use AI in the next six months. Firms with 250+ employees: **37%**. Firms with 100–249 employees: **32%**. Firms with four or fewer employees: **under 20%**. Use rose among firms with 20+ employees but **did not change significantly among firms with fewer than 20 employees**. By sector: Information 39.7%, Finance & Insurance 33.9%, Retail 14%; the national rate was 19.8% as of May 3, 2026 — [US Census Bureau, May 2026](https://www.census.gov/library/stories/2026/05/ai-use-businesses.html)
- Methodology caveat: in late 2025 the BTOS question changed from AI use "in producing goods or services" to use "in any of its business functions." Before that change, the adoption rate grew 68% in the year ending September 2025 — [Federal Reserve FEDS Note, Apr 3 2026](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/monitoring-ai-adoption-in-the-u-s-economy-20260403.html)
- The Fed compares three measures. BTOS: about **18% of firms** as of year-end 2025. Real-Time Population Survey: about **41% of workers** use GenAI at work. Survey of Business Uncertainty: about **78% of the labor force** works at firms that have adopted AI (employment-weighted). Finance and professional services are about 30–33% in BTOS versus 62–63% in the RPS. Micro-firm adoption came in above what firm size alone would predict — [Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/monitoring-ai-adoption-in-the-u-s-economy-20260403.html)
- Goldman Sachs Research (May 2026) reports that BTOS adoption "rises to 19.8%" as of April 2026. I have this from the report title only; the page returned 403 — [GS Research](https://www.gspublishing.com/content/research/en/reports/2026/05/04/ef83dcd8-de76-45cd-b883-2a9e6cd9cc2a.html)

**Bank and vendor surveys [Tier 2; samples skew toward engaged owners]**
- Goldman Sachs 10,000 Small Businesses (1,256 program participants, Babson/David Binder, Jan 27 – Feb 4, 2026): **76% use AI**, 93% say it has had a positive impact, 84% cite efficiency, **only 14% say AI is fully embedded in core operations**, and **73% say more training and resources would help**. I have these figures from search snippets and Benzinga coverage; the press release page returned 403 — [Goldman Sachs press release](https://www.goldmansachs.com/pressroom/press-releases/2026/small-businesses-embrace-ai-but-need-training-and-support-to-fully-harness-it); [Benzinga](https://www.benzinga.com/markets/tech/26/03/51348089/small-businesses-race-to-adopt-ai-but-not-all-fully-integrate-it)
- Bluevine 2026 (942 US SMBs with 2–249 employees and $50K–$5M revenue, fielded Apr 7–9 2026 by Centiment):
  - Adoption: **74% use or test AI**; 20% have no current or planned use, down from 30% in 2025.
  - Barriers: **82% report at least one barrier**. Data security or privacy 33% (up from 23%), distrust of accuracy 31%, cost 24%, insufficient perceived value 20%. **78% don't fully trust AI for low-level tasks without oversight.**
  - Use cases: data analysis 39%, marketing/sales 37%, **operations automation 29%**, customer service 27%, finance 23%.
  - Returns and time saved: 52% report positive ROI; 48% save 4+ hours a week.
  - **Spend: 33% spend $0 on AI tools, 28% spend $25–99/month, 16% spend $100–249/month, 10% spend $250+/month.**
  - Source: [Bluevine via Stacker/KVIA](https://kvia.com/stacker-small-business/2026/07/16/3-4-of-small-businesses-dont-trust-ai-for-basic-tasks/); [Bluevine](https://www.bluevine.com/blog/small-business-ai-trends-report-2026)
- Other figures (**2025 data**): 68% of small businesses use AI per a QuickBooks-commissioned survey (2025), and 58% use GenAI per the US Chamber of Commerce (2025). These come via an aggregator blog — [Capsule CRM aggregator](https://capsulecrm.com/blog/small-business-ai-adoption-statistics/)
- Salesforce SMB Trends (**2024**, 3,350 SMBs under 200 employees): 91% of SMBs using AI say it boosted revenue [vendor-sponsored, likely inflated] — [aggregator: Capsule CRM](https://capsulecrm.com/blog/small-business-ai-adoption-statistics/)

### Inferences
- The gap between 20% (Census) and 75% (surveys) is mostly definitional: "an owner uses ChatGPT" versus "the firm uses AI in a business function," plus self-selected samples. For a services business, the relevant signal is the **roughly 14% "fully embedded" figure and the 73% who want training and support**. Those point to a large gap between trying AI and integrating it, which is exactly what agencies claim to fill.
- Bluevine's spending data is a warning: **61% of SMBs spend under $100/month on AI tools**. Budgets for $5K–$20K custom automation projects exist in only a minority of small firms. Firms with 20–249 employees (where Census shows adoption rising) and information and finance sectors look like the stronger buyers.
- Trust and security concerns (33%, 31%, 78%) suggest that human-in-the-loop design, data governance, and ongoing support are selling points, not just tool building.

### Gaps
- I did not find an SBA-published 2026 AI adoption statistic. The SBA Office of Advocacy mostly cites Census BTOS.
- The widely circulated "lack of understanding 62% / no in-house expertise 60%" barrier figures could not be traced to a primary source; I excluded them.
- The Fed Small Business Credit Survey AI questions were not checked.

## 3. Evidence of AI project failure and ROI problems, and implications for service providers

### Takeaway
The headline failure rates are real but narrowly defined. MIT NANDA's "95%" means **no measurable P&L impact within about six months**. Gartner's "40%+" is a *prediction* that agentic projects will be canceled by the end of 2027. McKinsey finds only **39% see any enterprise-level EBIT impact and about 6% are "high performers."** Buyers are becoming more ROI-skeptical, and "agent washing" is common.

### Cited Findings
- MIT NANDA, "The GenAI Divide" (July/Aug 2025, **2025 data**): 52 executive interviews, a survey of 153 leaders, and analysis of 300 public deployments. **95% of GenAI pilots delivered no measurable P&L impact**, despite an estimated $30–40B in enterprise investment. The successful minority tended to **buy from external vendors or partners** rather than build internally, focused on back-office use cases, and measured workflow change — [Fortune via Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/mit-report-95-generative-ai-105412686.html); [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/jasonsnyder/2025/08/26/mit-finds-95-of-genai-pilots-fail-because-companies-avoid-friction/); [analysis of what it measured](https://agentmodeai.com/the-mit-genai-pilot-failure-claim/)
- The MIT study has drawn methodological criticism: a small sample, a 6-month P&L window, and a definition of "failure" as no P&L impact — [Rob Lee Substack critique](https://robtlee73.substack.com/p/the-genai-divide-report-a-masterclass); [agentmodeai](https://agentmodeai.com/the-mit-genai-pilot-failure-claim/)
- Gartner (June 25, 2025): **over 40% of agentic AI projects will be canceled by the end of 2027** because of escalating costs, unclear business value, or inadequate risk controls. Gartner describes most such projects as early-stage, hype-driven PoCs, and flags "agent washing" (for example, chatbots or RPA rebranded as agents). Gartner also predicts that by 2028, 15% of day-to-day work decisions will be made autonomously and 33% of enterprise apps will include agentic AI — [Gartner press release](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-06-25-gartner-predicts-over-40-percent-of-agentic-ai-projects-will-be-canceled-by-end-of-2027); [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2025/06/29/ai_agents_fail_a_lot/)
- McKinsey State of AI (Nov 2025, **2025 data**): **62% are at least experimenting with agents and 23% are scaling agents somewhere**, but no more than 10% are scaling agents in any given function. **Only 39% report any enterprise-level EBIT impact**, usually under 5% of EBIT. **About 6% are "AI high performers"** (5%+ of EBIT from AI) — [McKinsey](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/quantumblack/our-insights/the-state-of-ai)
- Menlo (2025): only **16% of enterprise deployments and 27% of startup deployments are "true agents."** Most are fixed-sequence or routing workflows — [Menlo Ventures](https://menlovc.com/perspective/2025-the-state-of-generative-ai-in-the-enterprise/)
- Counterpoint: Menlo reports 47% of AI deals reach production, compared with 25% for SaaS — [Menlo Ventures](https://menlovc.com/perspective/2025-the-state-of-generative-ai-in-the-enterprise/)

### Inferences
- For service providers the picture cuts both ways. MIT found that externally sourced solutions succeed more often, and Goldman found only 14% of SMBs have AI "fully embedded." Both support demand for implementation partners. But the failure narrative makes buyers demand measurable ROI, which favors fixed-scope, outcome-priced, back-office projects (document processing, lead handling, reporting) over vague "AI transformation" pilots.
- Most real "agents" in production are deterministic workflows (Menlo: only 16% of enterprise deployments are true agents). Selling reliable workflow automation with LLM steps may be more defensible than selling autonomous agents.

### Gaps
- There is no SMB-specific study of AI project failure rates; all of the above is enterprise data.
- A 2026 update of the MIT NANDA study was not found.

## 4. Saturation of the "AI automation agency" (AAA) model

### Takeaway
The AAA model is heavily "course-ified." Liam Ottley's free Skool community alone has **about 334K members (August 2026)**, and freelance marketplaces show crowded entry tiers with falling reply rates. The low end (simple Zapier/Make/n8n builds at $25–80/hour) is commoditized. The field's own educators say that simply "building automations" is losing value.

### Cited Findings
- Liam Ottley popularized the "AI Automation Agency" model. His free **AI Automation Agency Hub on Skool has 333,800+ members as of August 2026** (from a third-party review; I did not verify the count directly on Skool) — [AI Funnel Insider review](https://aifunnelinsider.com/ai-automation-agency-hub-review-2026/); [Skool community page](https://www.skool.com/ai-automation-agency-hub-8466)
- Nick Saraev's paid Maker School (n8n/Claude agency building) costs **$184/month (about $129/month on annual billing) and has about 2,210 members**. Saraev has written that "we are fast-approaching a world where 'building automations' will be considered as quaint as hand-stitching dresses in the 1850s" — [Self-Taught AI Builder's Playbook, Substack](https://automationalchemy.substack.com/p/the-self-taught-ai-builders-playbook) (secondary)
- Upwork rates (2026): n8n freelancers commonly bill **$40–100/hour**, and senior agent/API specialists charge **$125–250+/hour** — [Upwork hire page](https://www.upwork.com/hire/n8n-experts/); other estimates give $25–80/hour for Make/n8n work — [Ciela AI](https://ciela.ai/blogs/freelance-ai-automation-rates-2026) [low-reliability blogs]
- GigRadar Upwork analysis (2026) [vendor blog, moderate reliability]: AI work on Upwork grew **about 50% YoY**, but the AI/ML subcategory is "over-fished," with an **agency proposal reply rate of 7.21%, below the platform mean**. Price-only competitors in the entry tier often earn below minimum wage once unpaid proposal time is counted — [GigRadar](https://gigradar.io/blog/upwork-market-report-2026)
- Commentators argue that platform vendors (Zapier, UiPath, and others) are moving into the space and making it "saturated" for beginners — [ippei.com AAA Accelerator review](https://ippei.com/aaa-accelerator/) [opinion; affiliate-style review, low reliability]
- Model and platform shift: Menlo's 76% bought versus 24% built split for enterprise AI indicates buyers increasingly buy packaged tools rather than commission custom builds — [Menlo Ventures](https://menlovc.com/perspective/2025-the-state-of-generative-ai-in-the-enterprise/)

### Inferences
- If even 1% of the roughly 334K Skool members actively run agencies, that is about 3,000+ micro-agencies competing mainly in cold outreach to SMBs. This is an inference; no source gives a count of operating agencies.
- Commoditization is concentrated at the "connect tool A to tool B" layer. Pricing power appears to remain for (a) vertical specialization, (b) complex integrations with LLM agents or APIs ($125–250/hour tier), and (c) ongoing managed service and governance, which matches the SMB trust and security concerns in section 2.
- Much AAA content is itself a business model (courses and communities selling to aspiring agency owners). Success claims from course sellers should be treated as marketing.

### Gaps
- **No reliable count exists of AI automation agencies**, nor any data on their survival or revenue distribution. I found no WSJ, Bloomberg, or The Information investigation quantifying AAA saturation in this pass.
- Skool member counts are from third-party reviews and are not independently verified.

## 5. Which buyer segments spend most (SMB vs mid-market vs enterprise; by industry)

### Takeaway
Spending is heavily skewed toward **large enterprises and toward information, finance/insurance, and professional services**. Among small firms, adoption is rising mainly in the **20–249 employee** band, and most micro-businesses spend under $100/month on AI.

### Cited Findings
- Adoption by firm size (Census, December 2025 to May 2026): 250+ employees 37%, 100–249 employees 32%, and fewer than 20 employees under 20% and flat — [Census](https://www.census.gov/library/stories/2026/05/ai-use-businesses.html)
- Industry (Census, May 2026): **Information 39.7%, Finance & Insurance 33.9%**, national 19.8%, Retail 14% — [Census](https://www.census.gov/library/stories/2026/05/ai-use-businesses.html). The Fed notes that finance and professional services "stand out" (30–33% in BTOS) — [Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/monitoring-ai-adoption-in-the-u-s-economy-20260403.html)
- Enterprise vertical AI spend (Menlo, 2025): **healthcare is the largest vertical at $1.5B** of $3.5B. Among departmental budgets, coding took $4.0B (55%), then IT at 10%, marketing 9%, customer success 9%, design 7%, and HR 5% — [Menlo Ventures](https://menlovc.com/perspective/2025-the-state-of-generative-ai-in-the-enterprise/)
- Enterprise services: Accenture alone reports about $1.1B of AI revenue per quarter across 1,300+ clients (Q1 FY26), which shows where large services dollars concentrate — [Luminix summary](https://www.useluminix.com/reports/company-overviews/what-will-ai-do-to-accenture-s-business/source/0)
- SMB wallet (Bluevine, 2–249 employees): 33% spend $0, **only 10% spend $250+/month** on AI tools — [Bluevine via KVIA](https://kvia.com/stacker-small-business/2026/07/16/3-4-of-small-businesses-dont-trust-ai-for-basic-tasks/)

### Inferences
- The best-fit target for a two-person agency is likely **lower mid-market firms (about 20–250 employees) in professional services, finance/insurance, healthcare administration, and real estate**. They have a rising adoption rate, cognitive and document-heavy workflows, and budgets. Enterprises spend the most but buy from large systems integrators and packaged vendors, and micro-SMBs have little budget.

### Gaps
- There is no public breakdown of AI *services* spending by company size (SMB, mid-market, enterprise). Gartner's and IDC's services splits by size were not accessible.
- There are no reliable data on typical SMB project budgets for custom automation beyond vendor blog estimates.
