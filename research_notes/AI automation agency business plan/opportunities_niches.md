# Opportunities, Niches, Competitive Threats, and the Services-to-Software Path for AI Automation (as of Sept 2026, US focus)

Source-quality note for the report writer: many "missed call" and "speed-to-lead" statistics circulate mainly through vendor blogs (AI receptionist sellers). Where only vendor or aggregator sources were found, this is flagged. Peer-reviewed / primary sources are marked [PRIMARY].

## 1. Which verticals show the strongest ROI and demand for automation?

### Takeaway
The best-evidenced demand is in **phone-heavy, lead-driven SMB verticals** (home services, dental, legal intake), where unanswered calls are well documented and directly cost revenue, and in **labor-constrained back offices** (accounting, property management, healthcare documentation). But the core home-services "missed-call AI receptionist" use case is now being built into the vertical SaaS platforms themselves, which erodes the standalone agency opportunity there.

### Cited Findings
**Home services (HVAC, plumbing, trades)**
- ServiceTitan's "State of AI in the Trades 2026" (Thrive Analytics survey of 1,000+ contractors, Oct 23–Nov 12, 2025) found: 54% very or somewhat willing to invest in AI in the next 1–3 years; only 12% have "already embedded AI"; 35% have not used AI at all. 59% of AI adopters use AI built into software they already have, and 42% of those also use outside tools like ChatGPT or Claude. Top barriers: lack of training (44%), integration complexity (44%), difficulty understanding the tools (38%), unclear ROI (37%). Top uses: admin (59%), marketing and sales (51%) — [ServiceTitan blog [PRIMARY survey summary]](https://www.servicetitan.com/blog/2026-ai-in-the-trades-report-takeaways)
- Missed-call rates quoted for contractors vary widely: "up to 27%", "40–60%", and a "62% missed call rate" attributed to a 2024 ServiceTitan study of 50,000+ lines. All come from vendor or aggregator pages, and I could not verify the 62% figure against a primary ServiceTitan source — [Nextiva](https://www.nextiva.com/blog/ai-receptionists-for-home-service-businesses.html); [CallJolt](https://calljolt.com/blog/guides/home-service-business-missed-call-statistics); [Higrovi "4.2M calls analyzed" report (vendor)](https://www.higrovi.com/blog/contractor-missed-call-report-2026)
- Vendor case study: Bonney Plumbing reported 60% fewer missed calls and an 11% increase in bookings after adopting ServiceTitan Contact Center Pro — [ServiceTitan Contact Center Pro page](https://www.servicetitan.com/features/pro/contact-center)
- Labor shortage: ACHR News is cited for a shortage of about 110,000 HVAC technicians, with about 25,000 leaving each year and a possible 225,000 gap within five years — [ServiceTitan HVAC shortage blog](https://www.servicetitan.com/blog/hvac-technician-shortage); [SMACNA](https://www.smacna.org/news/news-archive/article/2025/10/09/beat-the-hvac-technician-shortage)

**Dental / medical practices**
- Dental practices are said to miss about 20–38% of calls in business hours; "only 68% of new-patient calls answered"; "67% of callers who can't reach an office call another practice." All come from vendor or consultant blogs, and none of these pages gives a primary methodology — [Peerlogic](https://www.peerlogic.com/post/turning-missed-dental-phone-calls-into-profit); [Scheduling Institute](https://schedulinginstitute.com/blog/hidden-cost-of-missed-dental-calls/); [Practice by Numbers](https://practicenumbers.com/blog/cost-of-dental-practice-missed-calls/)
- Healthcare documentation is a proven, venture-validated vertical. Abridge (ambient AI medical scribe) raised $300M at a $5.3B valuation in June 2025, four months after a $2.75B round, and Sacra estimates about $100M ARR by May 2025 — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/06/24/in-just-4-months-ai-medical-scribe-abridge-doubles-valuation-to-5-3b/); [Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/abridge/)

**Legal intake**
- Clio's 2024 Legal Trends Report (secret-shopper study): law firms answering a prospective client's call fell from 56% (2019) to 40% (2024). Only 20% of firms that missed the call returned it, 48% were unreachable by phone, and 64% of prospects got no follow-up at all — [Clio](https://www.clio.com/blog/highlights-from-2024-legal-trends-report/); [2Civility summary](https://www.2civility.org/2024-clio-legal-trends-report-fixing-the-first-impression-problem-for-law-firms/)

**Real estate / property management**
- EliseAI (leasing and resident communications for apartment operators) raised $250M at a $2.2B valuation in Aug 2025 after passing $100M ARR in early 2025. It reportedly announced $200M ARR in June 2026 and was reported in Aug 2026 to be in talks to raise $300M at $3.7B (a16z and Bessemer). The talks are reported, not confirmed — [SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2025/08/20/property-management-startup-eliseai-nabs-250m-2-2b-valuation/); [Tech Funding News](https://techfundingnews.com/report-ai-landlord-eliseai-targets-3-7b-valuation-with-300m-raise-from-a16z-and-bessemer/)

**Accounting / bookkeeping**
- More than 300,000 accountants left the field in 2019–2022 (WSJ, as cited). The AICPA calls it a "pipeline crisis," accounting enrollments fell another 6.6%, and BLS projects about 115,300 openings a year — [Ramp](https://ramp.com/blog/accountant-shortage); [CPA Journal](https://www.cpajournal.com/2025/08/15/the-accounting-profession-is-in-crisis-3/); [ACCWire](https://www.accwire.com/accountant-shortage/)
- Sequoia sizes accounting/audit at $50–80B as an "autopilot" target — [Sequoia](https://sequoiacap.com/article/services-the-new-software)

**Insurance, recruiting, logistics, healthcare RCM (from VC sizing)**
- Sequoia's "Services: The New Software" (March 2026) gives these TAMs: insurance brokerage $140–200B; supply chain/procurement $200B+; recruitment/staffing $200B+; management consulting $300–400B; accounting/audit $50–80B; healthcare revenue cycle $50–80B; claims adjusting $50–80B — [Sequoia](https://sequoiacap.com/article/services-the-new-software)

### Inferences
- Legal intake has the strongest primary evidence of an unmet responsiveness need (Clio secret-shopper data), and high case values (e.g., personal injury) make willingness to pay plausible. It is a strong agency niche, especially because legal practice-management vendors have not (in what I found) bundled AI receptionists as aggressively as home-services platforms have.
- Home services has huge demand, but the platforms (ServiceTitan, Jobber, Housecall Pro) now ship native AI receptionists (see Q3). That leaves agencies the jobs of implementing, optimizing, and integrating these tools rather than selling a standalone receptionist. The 44% "lack of training" and 44% "integration complexity" barriers point to an enablement/implementation opportunity.
- Labor shortages (accounting, HVAC) strengthen the ROI case: automation offsets roles that can't be hired, not only cost cutting. That is a more durable sales story.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed or primary-methodology source was found for dental or home-services missed-call rates. Treat these as directional.
- No reliable independent data on SMB willingness to pay (price points) for AI receptionists beyond vendor list prices (e.g., Jobber at $29/mo for 30 conversations — see Q3).
- Insurance agencies, recruiting, and e-commerce support were not researched in depth beyond VC TAM sizing.

## 2. Which use cases have measurable ROI?

### Takeaway
Customer-support AI has the strongest evidence. There is rigorous academic evidence for AI assisting agents (+14% productivity, +34% for novices). Vendor-reported autonomous resolution rates are about 45–65%. Klarna shows both the upside and the danger of over-automating. Speed-to-lead has a clear, but old (2007) and heavily recycled, evidence base. At the enterprise level, the MIT NANDA "95% of pilots fail" finding is widely cited but methodologically contested.

### Cited Findings
- **Brynjolfsson, Li & Raymond, "Generative AI at Work"** (QJE vol. 140, no. 2, May 2025; NBER w31161): in a staggered rollout of an AI assistant across 5,179 customer-support agents, issues resolved per hour rose 14% on average and 34% for novice/low-skilled workers, with minimal gain for the most experienced. Customer sentiment and employee retention also improved — [QJE [PRIMARY]](https://academic.oup.com/qje/article/140/2/889/7990658); [NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w31161)
- **Klarna**: launched an OpenAI-powered assistant in Feb 2024, which handled 2.3M chats (two-thirds of volume) in its first month and was said to do "the work of 700 full-time agents." In May 2025 CEO Sebastian Siemiatkowski told Bloomberg the company "went too far," said cost-driven automation had produced "lower quality," and began rehiring humans in an "Uber-style" flexible model for complex cases (disputes, hardship). A later update (reported Q3 2025) put AI at the equivalent of 853 agents and about $60M in annual savings. These later figures come from secondary sources — [CX Dive](https://www.customerexperiencedive.com/news/klarna-reinvests-human-talent-customer-service-AI-chatbot/747586/); [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/quickerbettertech/2025/05/18/business-tech-news-klarna-reverses-on-ai-says-customers-like-talking-to-people/); [Perspective AI case study (secondary)](https://getperspective.ai/blog/klarna-ai-customer-service-replacing-700-agents-conversational-ai-case-study)
- **HubSpot Breeze Customer Agent**: HubSpot reports 65% of conversations resolved and 39% faster resolution across 8,000+ activations, with top teams at 90%+ — [HubSpot](https://www.hubspot.com/products/artificial-intelligence/use-cases/resolve-support-tickets) (vendor-reported)
- **Intercom Fin**: priced at $0.99 per resolution (50/month minimum, plus seats). One third-party review puts real-world resolution at 45–53% — [Gleap](https://www.gleap.io/blog/intercom-fin-ai-pricing-2026); [CloneDesk (competitor, treat with caution)](https://clonedesk.ai/blog/intercom-fin-limitations)
- **Speed-to-lead**: the 2007 MIT/InsideSales.com Lead Response Management study (Oldroyd; six companies, 15,000+ leads, 100,000+ call attempts) found that contacting within 5 minutes versus 30 minutes gave 100x the contact rate and 21x the qualification rate. The separate 2011 HBR audit of 2,241 companies found a 42-hour average response time and 23% of firms never responding. The studies are old and often misattributed — [AInora summary (secondary)](https://ainora.lt/blog/lead-response-time-statistics-every-study-2026); [LeadAngel](https://www.leadangel.com/blog/operations/lead-response-time/)
- **Enterprise ROI skepticism**: MIT Project NANDA's "The GenAI Divide: State of AI in Business 2025" (July/Aug 2025; 52 interviews, 153 surveyed leaders, 300 public deployments) claimed that 95% of GenAI pilots delivered no measurable P&L impact, blaming brittle workflows, weak contextual learning, and poor fit with operations. The small sample and definitions have been criticized — [Virtualization Review](https://virtualizationreview.com/articles/2025/08/19/mit-report-finds-most-ai-business-investments-fail-reveals-genai-divide.aspx); [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/jasonsnyder/2025/08/26/mit-finds-95-of-genai-pilots-fail-because-companies-avoid-friction/); critique: [Rob Lee Substack](https://robtlee73.substack.com/p/the-genai-divide-report-a-masterclass)
- **Contested point**: the same NANDA report reportedly found that buying from specialized vendors and partnerships succeeds more often than internal builds. This supports the external-implementer model, but I did not verify it from the primary PDF — [Virtualization Review](https://virtualizationreview.com/articles/2025/08/19/mit-report-finds-most-ai-business-investments-fail-reveals-genai-divide.aspx)

### Inferences
- The most defensible ROI claims for an agency are (a) human-plus-AI support augmentation (backed by the QJE study), (b) after-hours and overflow call capture tied to booked-job revenue, and (c) speed-to-lead automation. Klarna and NANDA argue for selling hybrid designs with human escalation, not "replace your staff" pitches.
- Outcome pricing (Intercom at $0.99/resolution, HubSpot at $0.50/resolved conversation) is now the vendor norm for support agents. That creates a low per-unit price anchor agencies must beat or build on top of.

### Gaps
- No rigorous independent study was found on document-processing time savings for SMBs (e.g., bookkeeping, legal document review). Evidence is mostly vendor case studies.
- Klarna's 2025–26 figures come from secondary sources. The primary Klarna earnings or press releases were not fetched.

## 3. Competitive threats: big-vendor native AI, no-code agent builders, vertical SaaS

### Takeaway
The squeeze is real and speeding up. CRM and productivity giants are shipping agents with outcome-based or credit pricing. Vertical SaaS platforms (ServiceTitan, Jobber, Housecall Pro) now bundle AI receptionists and assistants. Contractors say they prefer AI built into software they already use. No-code builders (n8n, Zapier Agents) lower the barrier to entry but are also the tooling agencies build with. The generic "build a chatbot/automation" agency is being commoditized. The remaining value is in integration across messy systems, change management, and vertical domain expertise.

### Cited Findings
- **Salesforce Agentforce** (Q2 FY27, reported Aug 26, 2026): Agentforce ARR above $1.5B (+240% YoY); Agentforce plus Data 360 ARR about $3.9B; accounts with agents in production +70% quarter over quarter; 3.2B "Agentic Work Units"; outcome-based deals and Agentforce One Edition bookings more than doubled quarter over quarter — [Salesforce press release [PRIMARY]](https://www.salesforce.com/news/press-releases/2026/08/26/fy27-q2-earnings/)
- **Microsoft Copilot Studio**: agents billed in Copilot Credits ($200 per 25,000 credits/month, or $0.01 per credit pay-as-you-go). Microsoft Agent 365 went GA May 1, 2026 as a $15/user/month add-on — [Microsoft pricing](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365-copilot/pricing/copilot-studio); [CloudZero](https://www.cloudzero.com/blog/copilot-studio-pricing/)
- **HubSpot Breeze**: Customer Agent moved from $1.00 per conversation to $0.50 per resolved conversation (April 14, 2026). The Prospecting Agent also moved to pay-when-complete — [HubSpot company news [PRIMARY]](https://www.hubspot.com/company-news/hubspots-customer-agent-and-prospecting-agent-now-you-pay-when-the-task-is-complete); [CMSWire](https://www.cmswire.com/customer-experience/hubspot-shifts-breeze-ai-agents-to-pay-per-result-pricing/)
- **OpenAI AgentKit / Agent Builder**: launched Oct 6, 2025 at DevDay as a visual drag-and-drop multi-agent builder — [OpenAI [PRIMARY]](https://openai.com/index/introducing-agentkit/). One search summary said OpenAI announced on June 3, 2026 that it would wind down Agent Builder and Evals by Nov 30, 2026. **Unverified; I did not find a primary source. The writer should treat this as unconfirmed.**
- **Zapier Agents**: agents across 9,000+ apps. They are personal/internal automations and cannot be embedded as customer-facing experiences — [Zapier](https://zapier.com/agents); [Zapier help](https://help.zapier.com/hc/en-us/articles/24393442652557-Build-an-agent-in-Zapier-Agents)
- **n8n**: $180M Series C at $2.5B (Oct 2025, led by Accel). Reported valuation about $5.2B after a strategic investment from SAP (May 2026), according to aggregators, not a primary source — [n8n blog [PRIMARY]](https://blog.n8n.io/series-c/); [Latka (aggregator)](https://getlatka.com/companies/n8nio)
- **Vertical SaaS native AI**:
  - Jobber Receptionist (GA Aug 2025; 200,000+ conversations by launch): $29/mo add-on for 30 conversations, unlimited on the Plus plan — [PR Newswire [PRIMARY]](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/jobber-launches-ai-powered-receptionist-to-answer-calls-and-texts-for-busy-home-service-businesses-302531125.html); [Jobber](https://www.getjobber.com/features/ai-receptionist/)
  - Housecall Pro CSR AI: answers calls, books jobs, captures leads. 24/7 call answering is a paid add-on; other AI assistants are included in all plans — [Housecall Pro](https://www.housecallpro.com/features/ai-team/csr-ai/)
  - ServiceTitan: Atlas AI sidekick and Titan Intelligence (dispatch, reports, workflows), plus Contact Center Pro, which is native to ServiceTitan rather than a third-party add-on — [ServiceTitan Pantheon 2025](https://www.servicetitan.com/press/servicetitan-introducing-the-next-evolution-of-ai-at-pantheon-2025-keynote); [Contact Center Pro](https://www.servicetitan.com/features/pro/contact-center)
  - 59% of contractor AI adopters use AI embedded in existing software — [ServiceTitan survey](https://www.servicetitan.com/blog/2026-ai-in-the-trades-report-takeaways)
- **Model-provider threat to startups** (Foundation Capital, "When model providers eat everything"): "the model provider that powers you can also turn around and steamroll you." The recommended moats are high-exception workflows, proprietary feedback loops and evaluators, owning the "duct-tape" execution layer across legacy systems, embedded domain experts, and speed — [Foundation Capital](https://foundationcapital.com/ideas/when-model-providers-eat-everything-a-survival-guide-for-service-as-software-startups) (the fetch tool reported publication as Sep 26, 2026; not independently confirmed)

### Inferences
- **How much this squeezes agencies**: generic builds (FAQ chatbots, simple Zaps, standalone AI receptionists for businesses already on ServiceTitan or Jobber) will face price anchors of $0.50–$0.99 per resolution or $29/month add-ons. Agency value moves toward (1) implementing and configuring native tools (Agentforce, Copilot Studio, and HubSpot all need partners), (2) cross-system integration where no single vendor reaches, (3) SMB segments on fragmented or no-platform stacks (e.g., legal intake, small dental groups), and (4) ongoing optimization and managed service.
- Salesforce, Microsoft, and HubSpot moving to consumption and outcome pricing creates an opening: clients need help forecasting costs and governing usage, which is a billable advisory service.

### Gaps
- No quantitative data found on agency revenue declines or margin compression attributable to native AI features.
- The status of OpenAI Agent Builder in 2026 is unverified.
- Legal and dental practice-management vendors' native AI intake features (Clio, Dentrix, etc.) were not researched.

## 4. VC theses on AI-enabled services / service-as-software, and agency-to-platform examples

### Takeaway
Top VCs agree that AI's biggest prize is the services budget, not the software budget. Sequoia puts the ratio at $6 of services spend for every $1 of software; Foundation Capital sizes the opportunity at $4.6T; General Catalyst has committed $1.5B to AI-enabled rollups. The "autopilot" or "sell the work" model, with outcome pricing, is the favored end state. For a small agency, the realistic path is to productize in one vertical, price on outcomes, capture workflow data, and then turn repeatable deliverables into software. Rollups are a capital-intensive, VC-scale route.

### Cited Findings
- **Sequoia, "Services: The New Software"** (Julien Bek, March 2026): "The next $1T company will be a software company masquerading as a services firm." Six dollars are spent on services for every one on software. Copilots sell tools to professionals (e.g., Harvey to law firms); autopilots sell outcomes directly to end customers (e.g., Crosby drafting NDAs for companies), and autopilots capture larger budgets. The recommended wedge is "already-outsourced, intelligence-heavy tasks," then expanding into judgment work. Examples named: Anterior, Strala, Tacto, Juicebox — [Sequoia [PRIMARY]](https://sequoiacap.com/article/services-the-new-software); [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/josipamajic/2026/04/01/sequoia-says-ai-will-kill-software-tools-by-becoming-the-work/)
- **Foundation Capital, "Service-as-Software"** (Joanne Chen et al., 2024): AI automates the whole job rather than assisting with it, for a $4.6T opportunity ($2.3T in software-addressable salaries plus $2.3T in outsourced services, per Gartner) — [Foundation Capital](https://foundationcapital.com/ideas/the-ai-hype-600b-question-or-4-6t-opportunity); [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/joannechen/2024/04/29/ai-leads-a-service-as-software-paradigm-shift/)
- **General Catalyst, AI-enabled rollups ("Creation" strategy)**: $1.5B committed. GC mapped 70 service categories and found 10 where AI can automate 30–70% of the work, grouped into customer support and communication, data entry and processing, content generation, and early-stage reasoning and analysis. Portfolio: Eudia (legal), Titan (IT services/MSPs), Long Lake, Dwelly (UK lettings), Crescendo (AI-native contact center). Long Lake agreed to take American Express Global Business Travel private for $6.3B (reported May 2026), the strategy's largest deal. GC frames this as "AI transformation and product reinvention, not a PE roll-up" — [General Catalyst [PRIMARY]](https://www.generalcatalyst.com/stories/the-future-of-services); [PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/news/articles/general-catalysts-6-3b-amex-deal-puts-its-ai-roll-up-strategy-on-display)
- **Bessemer, State of AI 2025**: "Supernovas" reach about $40M ARR in year 1 and $125M in year 2, with about 25% gross margins and $1.13M ARR per FTE. "Shooting Stars" reach about $3M ARR in year 1 and $103M by year 4, with about 60% margins. "Vertical AI is the new SaaS," with "technophobic" industries adopting quickly when ROI is proven from day one — [BVP [PRIMARY]](https://www.bvp.com/atlas/the-state-of-ai-2025)
- **a16z**: reported to have documented, in its 2025 BPO work, that outsourced functions unbundle first and pricing moves from seats to outcomes. This comes from a secondary summary; the a16z primary essay was not fetched — [Yahoo Finance summary](https://finance.yahoo.com/sectors/technology/articles/ai-native-agencies-sell-outcomes-155608681.html)
- **Vertical AI "services-like" outcomes at scale**: EliseAI (property management, ~$200M ARR reported June 2026) and Abridge (clinical documentation, ~$100M ARR by May 2025, est.) show that single-vertical, workflow-owning AI can reach venture scale — [SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2025/08/20/property-management-startup-eliseai-nabs-250m-2-2b-valuation/); [Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/abridge/)
- **Agency to platform (SMB white-label route)**: GoHighLevel "SaaS Mode" lets agencies resell a white-labeled CRM/automation platform at $97–$297/client/month against a $497/month wholesale plan. Promoters claim margins rise from 30–50% (services) to 50–70%. **These sources are affiliate/promotional and the figures are anecdotal** — [GHL Experts](https://www.ghlexperts.com/agency-saas/scale-your-agency-with-ghl-saas-mode-and-snapshots); [Pintox](https://pintox.com/blog/gohighlevel/ghl-saas-mode/)
- Commentary warns that when "Sequoia, a16z, and YC all publish the same framework in the same quarter," the easy narrative is already priced in — [Yahoo Finance summary](https://finance.yahoo.com/sectors/technology/articles/ai-native-agencies-sell-outcomes-155608681.html)

### Inferences
- A practical staged path for a small agency: (1) pick one vertical and one workflow, such as legal intake or after-hours dispatch; (2) productize it as fixed scope and fixed price; (3) move to managed service with outcome pricing (per booked appointment, qualified lead, or resolved ticket); (4) turn the reused components (prompts, evals, integrations, dashboards) into a multi-tenant product; (5) optionally white-label or license to other agencies. Foundation Capital's moats (exception handling, feedback data, the execution layer) map directly onto what an embedded agency naturally accumulates.
- The "AI-enabled rollup" (buying traditional service firms and automating them) is the VC-scale version of the same path. It needs acquisition capital, but it validates the idea that owning the service P&L beats selling tools.
- Bessemer's Supernova margins (~25%) suggest that even celebrated AI companies have services-like gross margins. The "services agency versus software" margin gap may be narrower than traditional SaaS comparisons imply.

### Gaps
- No well-documented case study was found of an independent AI automation agency (not VC-backed) that became a venture-scale SaaS company. The evidence is mostly promotional GHL anecdotes.
- The a16z primary essay on AI-native services/BPO was not retrieved.

## 5. Emerging opportunities in 2026: agentic workflows, MCP, mid-market implementation, governance, training

### Takeaway
MCP has become the de facto open standard for connecting agents to tools, now under a neutral Linux Foundation body. This makes "build MCP servers/connectors for a client's systems" a concrete service line. Governance demand is real but driven by regulation that keeps shifting (Colorado delayed again and scaled back; EU AI Act phasing in). Training and integration are the top stated adoption barriers among SMB contractors, which supports enablement services.

### Cited Findings
- **MCP**: Anthropic donated MCP to the Agentic AI Foundation (AAIF), a Linux Foundation directed fund co-founded by Anthropic, Block, and OpenAI and supported by Google, Microsoft, AWS, Cloudflare, and Bloomberg (Dec 2025). At that point MCP had 97M+ monthly SDK downloads, about 10,000 active servers, and client support in ChatGPT, Claude, Cursor, Gemini, Microsoft Copilot, and VS Code — [MCP blog [PRIMARY]](https://blog.modelcontextprotocol.io/posts/2025-12-09-mcp-joins-agentic-ai-foundation/); [Linux Foundation [PRIMARY]](https://www.linuxfoundation.org/press/linux-foundation-announces-the-formation-of-the-agentic-ai-foundation)
- **Consumption and outcome pricing is spreading** (Agentforce AWUs and outcome deals; HubSpot $0.50/resolution; Copilot credits) — see Q3 sources.
- **AI governance/regulation (US)**: the Colorado AI Act was delayed from Feb 1, 2026 to June 30, 2026 (SB25B-004, Aug 2025). On May 14, 2026, SB 189 delayed it again to Jan 1, 2027 and significantly scaled back its requirements. A federal magistrate judge stayed enforcement of the law on April 27, 2026 — [Hunton](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/colorado-ai-act-amended-and-effective-date-delayed); [Law and the Workplace](https://www.lawandtheworkplace.com/2026/05/major-developments-put-colorados-ai-law-on-ice-ahead-of-implementation/); [Troutman](https://www.troutmanprivacy.com/2026/04/colorado-attorney-general-delays-enforcement-of-colorado-ai-act/)
- ISO/IEC 42001 (AI management systems) demand is earliest in financial services, healthcare, HR tech, insurance, and B2B software vendors selling to enterprise or government, driven by procurement and the EU AI Act phasing in through 2026–2027. Big 4 firms (KPMG, Deloitte) already offer these services — [ISO](https://www.iso.org/standard/42001); [Deloitte](https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/services/consulting/articles/iso-42001-standard-ai-governance-risk-management.html); [Schellman](https://www.schellman.com/blog/iso-certifications/iso-42001-and-responsible-ai-governance)
- **Training/enablement demand**: among contractors, lack of training (44%) and integration complexity (44%) are the top AI adoption barriers, and 37% cite unclear ROI — [ServiceTitan survey](https://www.servicetitan.com/blog/2026-ai-in-the-trades-report-takeaways)
- **Mid-market implementation gap**: NANDA reports that only about 5% of custom enterprise AI tools reach production, citing brittle workflows and poor fit with operations. This is an argument for implementation partners, though the finding is contested (see Q2) — [Virtualization Review](https://virtualizationreview.com/articles/2025/08/19/mit-report-finds-most-ai-business-investments-fail-reveals-genai-divide.aspx)

### Inferences
- Promising 2026 service lines for a small US firm:
  - MCP connector and agent-integration work for mid-market clients on legacy or vertical systems (the "duct-tape layer")
  - Implementation and optimization of vendor-native agents (Agentforce, Copilot Studio, Breeze, ServiceTitan/Jobber AI) with ROI dashboards
  - AI usage and cost governance for consumption-priced agents
  - Staff training bundled with deployment
- US governance and compliance is a less certain standalone niche for a small agency right now: US state law is in flux, and the Big 4 and audit firms dominate ISO 42001.
